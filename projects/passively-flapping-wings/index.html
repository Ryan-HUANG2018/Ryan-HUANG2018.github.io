3DFSIInsect flight

Passively pitching flapping wings in hover

A hovering insect-like wing flaps back and forth on a prescribed stroke, but its pitch is not prescribed: a torsional spring at the hinge lets it pitch passively under aerodynamic, inertial and elastic torques. I mapped eight wing shapes against the hinge stiffness to find where lift and power economy are highest. Switch between a soft and a stiff hinge on the same wing to compare the lift coefficient over a stroke cycle and the vortex structures. From our 2023 Journal of Fluid Mechanics paper.

REYNOLDS NUMBER
300
CAUCHY NUMBER CH
0.05–0.6
WING SHAPES R̄₁
0.39–0.63 (8)
GRID
7.25 M (Δx = 0.04c)

Setup

A wing of span L and mean chord c flaps about a vertical axis through its root with the stroke angle φ = −(φA/2) cos(2πft), where the peak-to-peak amplitude φA = 140° matches that of fruit flies. The pitch angle θ about the leading edge is not prescribed. A torsional spring of stiffness Ks at the hinge pulls the wing back to vertical, and θ follows from the balance of the inertial, aerodynamic and elastic torques:

\[-J_{yy}\ddot{\theta} + J_{zy}\ddot{\phi}\cos\theta + \tfrac{1}{2}J_{yy}\dot{\phi}^{2}\sin 2\theta + T^{\mathrm{pitch}}_{\mathrm{aero}} - K_s(\theta-\theta_0) = 0\]

The hinge stiffness is measured by the Cauchy number Ch = ρφA2f2c3L2 / Ks, the ratio of aerodynamic to elastic torsional forces, varied from 0.05 (stiff) to 0.6 (soft). The wing shape is set by the radius of the first moment of area, r̄1. Eight planforms from 0.39 to 0.63 cover the range of most insect wings (0.43–0.6), with the area moving outboard as r̄1 grows. Span, mean chord, area and aspect ratio (AR = 2.96) are the same for every wing. The Reynolds number based on the mean wingtip speed is Re = 300, and the chord-based mass ratio is 1, close to that of fruit flies and honeybees.

The flow is solved with my D3Q19 multi-relaxation-time lattice Boltzmann solver and a feedback immersed boundary method. A multi-block grid in a 30c cube refines from 0.32c in the far field to 0.04c around the wing (7.25 million nodes). Six stroke cycles are run, and the averages are taken over the last one.

Results

Lift and power economy. Mean lift peaks for 0.05 < Ch ≤ 0.2, where the minimum angle of attack at midstroke is close to 45°. The highest lift (C̄L ≈ 0.67) comes from wings with more outboard area (r̄1 ≥ 0.565) and Ch ≈ 0.1. The power economy PE = C̄L / C̄P, the lift per unit aerodynamic power, is highest (≈ 0.71) for r̄1 ≈ 0.45 and Ch ≈ 0.2. That wing shape is close to a hawkmoth’s (r̄1 ≈ 0.47).

Power synchronisations. The best power economy appears when two synchronisations happen together:

  • the pitching elastic power of the spring is in anti-phase with the pitching aerodynamic and inertial powers; and
  • the flapping aerodynamic power is nearly in phase with the total input power of the system.

In these cases pitch reversal is timed with stroke reversal, so the input power passes effectively from flapping into passive pitching and into the fluid. At the optimum the flapping aerodynamic power is about three times the flapping inertial power.

Soft and stiff hinges. The two animations show the same wing (r̄1 = 0.63). With the stiff hinge (Ch = 0.05) the angle of attack at midstroke stays near 45°, and a large leading-edge vortex over the outboard wing gives strong suction and high lift. With the soft hinge (Ch = 0.45) the wing pitches much more and pitches up rapidly just before midstroke. During stroke reversal a strong negative pressure develops near the trailing edge on the pressure side, and the lift coefficient turns strongly negative near midstroke, lowering the mean lift.

These results suggest that matching wing shape to hinge stiffness lets insects, and flapping-wing robots, exploit passive pitching to support their weight while keeping power economy high.

Publication

Huang, Q., Bhat, S.S., Yeo, E.C., Young, J., Lai, J.C.S., Tian, F.-B. & Ravi, S. (2023). Power synchronisations determine the hovering flight efficiency of passively pitching flapping wings. Journal of Fluid Mechanics 974, A41.
