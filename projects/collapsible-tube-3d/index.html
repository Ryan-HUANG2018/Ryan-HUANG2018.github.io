Self-excited oscillations of a 3D collapsible tube

A three-dimensional thin-walled elastic tube, mounted between two rigid tubes and squeezed by an external pressure, collapses into a two-lobed shape and oscillates on its own. I simulated the full tube, without symmetry assumptions, from laminar flow (Re = 100) to turbulent flow (Re = 1000). The animations show the laminar case at Re = 200: switch between the wall deformation seen along the tube axis and the vortex structures coloured by pressure. From our 2024 Physics of Fluids paper.

Setup

A thin-walled elastic tube of radius R, length L = 5D and wall thickness h = 0.025D sits between two rigid tubes of diameter D, like the Starling resistor used in collapsible-tube experiments. An external pressure pe acts on the outside of the elastic section. A Hagen–Poiseuille profile with mean velocity U0 enters at the inlet, and the outlet pressure is pd = 0. A small perturbation in the external load starts the collapse into a two-lobed (mode-2) shape.

The fluid is solved with a lattice Boltzmann method on a uniform grid of 0.02D, with large eddy simulation (Smagorinsky model, Cs = 0.12) for the turbulent case. The tube wall is a linearly elastic solid undergoing large displacements (Poisson’s ratio 0.49), solved with explicit finite elements using 20-node reduced-integration hexahedra (C3D20R). A diffused-interface immersed boundary method couples the two, so the mesh never has to be regenerated as the tube collapses.

Results

Laminar flow. The tube stays at its equilibrium shape at Re = 100 and 160, and small oscillations die out at Re = 180. At Re = 200, periodic vortex shedding downstream of the throat drives small-amplitude, quasi-periodic self-excited oscillations, and strong transverse flow slowly rotates the tube deformation. Stress concentrations near the downstream end of the elastic tube create two regions of local wall thickening, at z/R = 7.25. Because the compressive load is cyclic, these regions may make the tube prone to fatigue failure.

Turbulent flow. At Re = 1000, the two-lobed throat produces two axial jets and a strong adverse pressure gradient where the tube reopens. The flow bifurcates and becomes asymmetric, and the two jets merge at about z/R = 22 in the downstream rigid tube. Vortex shedding from the throat drives large-amplitude, quasi-periodic oscillations of the wall. The jet merging adds small secondary oscillations to the pressure and streamwise velocity downstream. The spectra there follow the −5/3 Kolmogorov law, showing that the large eddy simulation captures the turbulence.

Publication

Huang, Q., Ji, X., Ma, J., Wang, L., Young, J. & Lai, J.C.S. (2024). Self-excited oscillations of three-dimensional collapsible tubes conveying both laminar and turbulent flows. Physics of Fluids 36(12), 121920.
